Home Pest Control: Protect Your Home Before and After Construction

You choose the flooring, the kitchen layout and the wall colours with great care. Yet one of the biggest threats to a new house is something you never get to choose: pests.

Termites eat wood from the inside, so the damage often goes unnoticed. Cockroaches and ants settle in wherever food and damp are easy to find, while mosquitoes breed in the smallest puddle. That is why good home pest control is not a one-time job. It works in two stages, one before the house is built and one after you move in.

This guide explains both stages in plain words, lists simple habits you can follow yourself, and helps you ask the right questions when you hire a professional.

Why Kerala homes face more pest trouble

Kerala gets heavy monsoon rain and stays humid for most of the year. Damp soil, wet walls and warm nights are exactly what termites and cockroaches look for. That is why pest control in Kerala works best when it is planned early, not after the damage shows.

A new house is at risk for three simple reasons:

  • The soil under the building may already have termite colonies in it.
  • Construction leaves behind wood scraps, wet corners and small gaps around pipes.
  • Doors, window frames, wardrobes and kitchen units give pests plenty to eat once you move in.

Pre-construction anti-termite treatment: protect the ground first

This treatment is done while the house is still being built, before floors and walls cover the soil. It is the only time a professional can reach the ground under and around your building directly. A trained technician applies an approved termite-control chemical to the soil, which forms a barrier that stops termites from creeping up into the structure.

When is it done? Usually in stages, as the work moves along:

  1. Foundation trenches and pits, before concrete goes in.
  2. Backfilled soil around the foundation and plinth.
  3. Soil under the floor, before the base layer is laid.
  4. Joints where floors meet walls, and gaps around pipes and drains.

Why it matters

  • Once the floors are laid, the soil underneath is out of reach. Fixing it later may mean drilling through finished floors.
  • Termites often stay hidden behind plaster, paint and furniture, so damage can grow for months before you notice.
  • Termite treatment for a new house is easier to plan and carry out when it is part of the build schedule than when it becomes a repair job.

Good to ask your builder:

  • Is the work done at each stage?
  • Will we get a written record with dates, areas covered, the product used and the warranty?
  • Does it follow the Indian Standard for anti-termite measures (IS 6313)?

Post-construction pest control: what to do after you move in

Even a well-treated house can attract pests later. Leaking pipes, food crumbs, blocked drains and gaps around doors and windows all invite them in. Post-construction pest control deals with pests that are already there and lowers the chance of them coming back.

Here is what to look for in each room:

Pest Common signs First step
Termites Thin mud tubes on walls, hollow-sounding wood, powdery wood dust, tiny discarded wings near windows Stop using the affected wood and call a professional for an inspection
Cockroaches Droppings like black pepper grains, egg cases, a musty smell in kitchens and under sinks Clean up food and grease, fix leaks, then book cockroach control for the kitchen and drains
Ants Steady trails to sweets or crumbs, small soil piles near cracks Wipe the trail, store food in sealed boxes, seal the entry gap
Mosquitoes Wrigglers in standing water, bites at dusk Empty plant trays, buckets and coolers every week

Proper termite control starts with an inspection to find where the pests enter and how far they have spread. Spraying a visible patch alone rarely solves it. The same is true for other pests: a good technician looks for the source, treats the affected areas, explains what caused the problem and returns for a follow-up check.

Safety note: Keep children and pets away during treatment and follow the technician's advice on when it is safe to re-enter. Cover food and utensils before the visit.

Simple habits that keep pests away

Chemical treatments form an essential defensive layer, but regular home maintenance keeps insects from settling in:

  • Keep dry: Fix leaking taps, kitchen sink pipes, and bathroom drains immediately. Moisture is the #1 attractant.
  • Store food tightly: Use air-tight containers for cereals, spices, and sugar. Never leave crumbs overnight.
  • Clear ventilation gaps: Maintain clear air around kitchen cabinets and wardrobe skirting to prevent damp air buildup.

How to choose a pest control professional

Always work with certified operators using government-approved formulations (CIB approved). Ask for written guarantees, follow-up schedules, and safety datasheets especially when treating kitchens and living spaces.

Frequently asked questions

When should anti-termite treatment be done for a new house?

Anti-termite treatment should be done during construction, before floors and walls cover the soil. Technicians usually treat the foundation trenches, the backfilled soil, the plinth area and the floor base in stages. Working at these points lets the chemical barrier reach the ground directly.

How long does pre-construction anti-termite treatment last?

Protection lasts for a limited period, and the exact time depends on the product, the soil and the site conditions. Leaks and soil disturbance can weaken the barrier sooner, so check the warranty in your treatment record. Regular inspections remain important even while the warranty is valid.

Can termites be treated after a house is built?

Yes, termites can be treated after construction. A professional first inspects the house to find the entry points and the spread, then treats the affected areas. The job is harder than treatment before construction, since the soil under finished floors is difficult to reach.

How often should home pest control be done?

A yearly inspection, ideally after the monsoon, suits most homes. Book a visit sooner if you notice mud tubes, wood dust, droppings or a sudden rise in insects. Early action keeps a small problem from turning into a costly repair.

Is pest control safe for children and pets?

Pest control is considered safe at home when trained professionals carry it out and the family follows the precautions. Children and pets should stay away during the visit and until the technician says it is safe to return. Covering food and utensils beforehand adds another layer of care.

What are the first signs of termites in a house?

The first signs are thin mud tubes on walls, hollow-sounding wood, powdery wood dust and tiny discarded wings near windows. These signals often appear around door frames, skirting and wooden furniture. Call a professional as soon as you notice any of them, since the damage usually continues out of sight.
